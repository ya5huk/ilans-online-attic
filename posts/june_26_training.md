It was an intense month, with a lot of competitions and a lot of training. 
I had some of my best training sessions this month, very good jumps (though they were either fouls or far from board),
and overall - just a very high quality month. 
The peak of the month was in June 25th, competing in the [Grand Slam](https://jgsm.org.il/) with [Luvo Manyonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luvo_Manyonga)! It's a shame I ended up having a cramp / pull in my hamstring after the 3rd jump, but I guess you either win or you learn.

So, let's break it into:

- training
- competitions
- hamstring cramp
- what's next

## Training

As written above - super fast, super intense month.

120*4-5 

## Competitions

Well, I competed twice more than I thought. Which in hindsight was probably a mistake. I thought I'll compete only on the 11th and maybe 25th (in addition to 3rd of June where I jumped 7m). So - 3 competitions in the best case in June, but in my mind it was really 2 - because I didn't think I'll make it to the Grand Slam. But, I ended up making it AND doing another competition on June 18th. 

So, basically since May 11th I competed every week up until June 25th. 7 weeks in a row. 

I'm glad the streak is over. I'm less glad it was forced (felt the hamstring cramp after the 3rd jump).

### June 3rd

I wrote about it in the last blog. I PB'd and jumped 7.00m (wind +2.0m/s).

### June 11th

Something felt off. Winds, 15 people jumping at the same pit. 4 jumps (and not 6). I felt like it could be better, but it is what it is. 6.72m.

### June 18th 

From the effort POV - I think my body jumped very well. From the results POV - I didn't put my leg on the board once except a not very good jump. 6.68m. I was stable on 6.60s without touching the board in a stadium that is known to produce lesser results than the Jerusalem one, where I jumped 7m.

### GRAND SLAM - June 25th

Look, this year is pretty much what I hoped of 2024 to be. It took me one year off the track and one insane year on it to achieve it. But I actually did - I competed at the Grand Slam Jerusalem. It is a *World Athletics Continental Tour Sliver* competition, there are usually a few very good athletes in each event. In LJ men it was Luvo Manyonga. He was an olympic silver medalist and world champion. It's actually insane to see athletes of this level in real life. On one hand, they are exactly like us. Super human. On the other, they are insanely strong. Not always they look like it though. But Luvo is strong. No doubt about it. 

OK so. My first international competition. There was 1,000+ people in the crowd which for Israel athletics is absolutely insane. Amazing atmosphere. Was very very fun. I came into that competition after 6 weeks of competing non-stop. I knew I was tired. I knew my muscles are tired, but I couldn't say no to the best competition out of all of these. Also, I found out I'm competing only like 2.5 days before. If I found out before, I wouldn't compete a week before.

I felt immortal in the warmup. I felt extremely fast. But when competition kicked in - I felt a bit less powerful for some reason. I still felt good, just not as good as in the warmup. (It is something that is actually happening to me alot. Going from feeling very good in the warmup to decent in comp). We were 8 competitiors, so we didn't wait long between jumps (which is awesome). 

I jumped a pretty bad 6.50 something jump. Then fouled two. Then felt an hamstring cramp. 

And that was about it.

#### The hamstring cramp 

Amazing how fast it switches from trying to jump the furthest you ever jumped to hoping I'll be able to continue competing and training after the competition. One twitch in the muscle. 

> And with larger context - I really went from being all-in to wanting to give up for months to running 12s in the 100m, then PBing twice and jumping lenghts I didn't think I'll ever jump, then competing in my first ever adult international competition and finally - hoping I just stay healthy. This was a long year. And to be honest - that's pretty much how track and field is, probably for most of athletes. Part of the sport.

Very humbling experience. 

And I'm super grateful that nothing serious (at least that's how it seems) happened. 

> I tried to backtrack and understand why did it happen to me. Tbh it can be so many things. My stomach was acting weird in the last days, it was my 7th consecutive week competition, The warmup, because of the call room and all was like 1hr and 20min. Maybe the technique is not effecient enough. I didn't sleep very well the night before. 
> 
> Anyways - I can't know for sure. 

I felt a cramp in my left upper hamstring somewhere along the last steps before jumping the 3rd jump. I fouled (unfortunetly. It might have been a good jump). And the second I stood up - I knew I have a serious decision to make. No one would take it for me. My coach said that only I know whether to continue. I even talked with some of my competitors, they recommended to listen to my body (which is super hard when you're at a competition you only dreamed you'll compete in).

I decided to skip the 4th, used a warming cream, did a lot of stretches and tested whether I can jump or not. In the 5th jump I decided to try to give a semi-serious jump. Just to test the waters. It's a shame I did it in front of a 1000 people but I needed to gain some security. Then, on the final jump I said "Aight, let's give it all". And gladly, nothing serious happened. I jumped a decent minus jump - 6.63m and that was it for the day. 

I ended that competition knowing *for sure* that I'm capable of jumping further than 7m. It's just a matter of time. 

> Also - I think I have a thing that's called runner's knee. I think I just competed way too much lately. We'll see what'll happen with it.

### Long Jump Technique 

I still didn't make "high pelvis, not-stopping on the board" thing a habit. 
I started doing it more this month, but it's still not close to where I would want it to be.

Last month, I wrote:

> I knew the jump was pretty good because I felt how I kept running at the board. The feeling was like being in the middle of a 100m and someone just put a wooden board right there, but I really ran up through the board and probably had a super horizontal jump.

I felt that a few more times this month - but it was either that I didn't step on the board or fouled.

I think this month I'll focus on staying less in the back, driving my knee more forward so my steps hit the ground at a better angle.
